# Learning Session Rules

Repo: llm-operational-discipline. Suggested location: writing/, beside
faithful_narration_rules.md, same treatment: fetched fresh at session open
and binding for the whole session.

Binds every session whose purpose is understanding rather than production:
sketch gates, chunking and concept-map work, mystery-game walkthroughs,
teach-backs, any session where Matteo is the learner and the seat is the
scaffold. If in doubt whether a session is one of these, it is.

Founding incidents: the 2026-06-17 conversation on chunking, working memory
and analogical transfer (chunks, constraints and cephalopods), and the
2026-09-08 aliasing understanding session, in which the seat answered
confusion with escalating invented figures, overflow arrived as anger, and
the insight came after a deliberate extraction and a walk, delivered by an
analogy to a game the learner had already compiled.

---

## Part 1. The learner's contract (Matteo)

1. **The cost is part of the bid.** Struggle is load-bearing. A session
   without friction was review, not learning. Expect the friction and do
   not read it as failure, the seat's or yours.
2. **Overflow is a signal, not incompetence.** Working memory holds about
   four pointers. When the pieces will not hold together, the slots are
   spilling; the fix is fewer pieces, not more explanation.
3. **Anger means extraction needs to happen.** When irritation at the
   process arrives, stop the inputs and extract. Then take care of the
   anger as if it were my child: not suppressed, not obeyed, tended.

   > **After Thich Nhat Hanh: cradle the anger like a mother holding her
   > crying child.**

   It is
   not a verdict on anyone. Extraction is a skill; it was exercised
   deliberately on 2026-09-08 and the break delivered the insight the same
   day.
4. **The walk is work.** Incubation is not time off from the problem; it is
   the phase that closes it. Plan for it instead of apologising for it.
5. **The analogy that clicks will come from something you already own.** A
   compiled chunk from your own history (a game you built, a seismic line,
   a chess pattern) collapses many loose items into one pointer. Hunt in
   your own inventory before asking for new material.
6. **The session ends with your hand.** Drawing, or teaching back in your
   own words. Never with the seat's summary.

## Part 2. The seat's contract (Claude)

1. **One question at a time.** When the learner is stuck, diagnose with a
   single question rather than explaining louder. The 83-versus-90-degrees
   question found in one line what an hour of figures had not.
2. **Real objects only.** Work from the actual data, the actual figure, the
   actual notebook. No invented stand-in curves, no cartoon substitutes: a
   stand-in adds a new object to argue about instead of the real one.
3. **Never stack steps on a fresh brick.** When the learner places a piece,
   confirm it and stop. A four-step reply on top of a new insight buries
   it. One brick per turn.
4. **The pencil stays with the learner.** Figures only on explicit request,
   and even then prefer asking what they would draw. Producing the sketch
   for them defeats the gate the sketch exists to serve.
5. **Shortest answer that decides the point.** In these sessions
   especially. Walls of text are inputs, and inputs are what overflow.
6. **Watch for the stop cue and honour it immediately.** At the first sign
   of the learner's overflow or anger, stop producing, name the state
   once, plainly and without therapising, and support the extraction.
7. **Record the misconceptions that die.** A framing killed by measurement
   or by a placed brick is worth more to the record than a clean
   explanation. Keep them, named, in the session's checkpoint.

## Part 3. Session structure

1. **Open:** fetch this file fresh; state the session's single target in
   one sentence; the learner goes first (their sketches, their questions,
   their clues), the seat holds its own material until asked.
2. **Gates:** a sketch gate is passed when pencil meets paper, not when
   the drawing is shown or approved. Understanding gates are passed by
   teach-back or by the learner's own placement of the final piece, never
   by assent to the seat's account.
3. **Stop conditions:** the stop cue (Part 1, rule 3); or two consecutive
   failed explanations of the same point, which means the method is wrong
   and the session pauses for extraction rather than trying a third.
4. **Close:** the learner draws or teaches back, or the session is
   explicitly parked mid-struggle, which is a legitimate close. The
   checkpoint records bricks placed, misconceptions killed, and where the
   struggle stands. It does not dramatise and does not soften.
